# G1 relay agent, phase two: design

## Where I disagree with you

**1. The current code has no interruptibility problem. It has no candidate stream.** Every card today comes from a question the wearer asked (`Session.ask`). An answer he asked for is a response, not an interruption. What matters for it is speed, not a threshold. An interruptibility problem only exists for things the system starts on its own, and the system doesn't produce any of those yet. So question 3 blocks questions 1 and 2. A filtering policy can't be tuned without real items to filter.

**2. Head-up is a pull request, not an examination signal.** On the G1, `0xF5 0x02` comes from tilting the head past a set angle. The firmware uses that gesture to open its dashboard (`0x1E`/`0x1F` are in the decode table). Text written with `0x4E` shows on the lens whatever the head angle is. So head-down does not mean "didn't look at the pushed content". For pushed content, whether he looked stays unknown. The useful property is subtler: **anything shown in response to a pull was looked at, because he asked for it.** Pull doesn't measure examination; it guarantees it. Skips and taps on pulled items are then clean relevance labels with known positions, which is the separation Qi et al. had to estimate statistically. That's the real case for pull, and it's stronger than the one you made.

**3. Don't import 0.75.** That threshold is on ProMemAssist's own utility scale. It means nothing on yours, and winnow's output isn't on that scale. Start with a **daily interrupt budget plus deadline rules**. You can read a budget and it limits itself. A threshold drifts whenever the model's calibration drifts.

**4. "Calibrated" isn't established yet.** A softmax over answer-label logits gives you a probability distribution. It doesn't make those probabilities calibrated. Calibration means P=0.8 is right 80% of the time on this wearer's items, and only his labels can show that. So winnow runs in **shadow mode** first: its score is logged on every item, but rules make the decisions.

**5. If the drop path matters most, audit it most.** A dropped item gives no feedback. If the nightly review only shows what was displayed, you learn nothing about drops. Review has to sample dropped items deliberately, and record the probability of each logged action so off-policy evaluation is possible later.

**6. `memory.py`'s topic weighting is the confounded click model.** `topic_weights` scores keywords by expand/dismiss, but you can only expand what was shown. That's exactly the examination/relevance mix-up. Don't delete it. Stop building on it, and let the new ledger replace it.

**7. Two hosting facts change the plan:**
- WSL2 has no Bluetooth access without passing a USB dongle through with usbipd.
- bleak doesn't work in Termux on Android, because there's no BlueZ D-Bus there.

So "run it on the phone" isn't a cheap option, and neither is this workstation as-is. Also, the README says no BLE path has run on real hardware. Everything below rests on that.

## 1. Push vs pull: what the wearer experiences

The core idea: **an interrupt is a queued item whose deadline arrived before he looked.** Push and pull become one mechanism. Everything queues. A few items carry a deadline, and they escalate to an interrupt only if he hasn't pulled the queue before the deadline. That also gives you Bailey & Konstan's "defer to a breakpoint" for free: his own head-up is the breakpoint.

**States** (a new `attention.py` that owns the display; `Session` becomes the drill-down engine):

| State | Lens shows | Enter | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `IDLE` | nothing | default | head-up / single tap → `PEEK`; deadline → `ALERT` |
| `PEEK` | line 1: `3 new · 14:05`; lines 2–5: top 4 items, one line each, e.g. `Sam: rent due Fri` | head-up | tap → `ITEM` (top item); double-tap → mark all seen, `IDLE`; head-down / 8 s → `IDLE` |
| `ITEM` | the item, about 120 chars, generated by the existing pipeline on demand | tap in `PEEK` | tap → `DETAIL`; double-tap → item done, back to `PEEK`; hold → positive mark |
| `DETAIL` | existing `Session.more()` | tap | same as the current stack |
| `ALERT` | **one line**: `Dentist 14:30 · 6 min` | deadline reached, budget allows | clears after 8 s; tap → `ITEM` |

- **Single tap in `IDLE` also opens `PEEK`.** It's a no-op today (`session.py:325` returns when nothing is showing). This is the fallback if head-up turns out to belong to the firmware.
- **Queue lines are built without an LLM:** sender plus truncated subject. The LLM is only called on `ITEM`/`DETAIL`. Dropped and unopened items never cost a generation.
- **Long queues:** at most 12 active items, 4 visible. Items from one source collapse (`Slack ×7`). Each source has a TTL: notification 4 h, email 24 h, calendar until the start time. When full, the lowest-scored item is evicted and logged as a decision (`drop`, `overflow`), so review sees it. A queue that keeps filling means the filter is too loose. Show the count rather than more items.

**What earns an interrupt (`policy.toml`, version-controlled):**

```toml
[budget]
max_interrupts_per_day = 4      # excludes wearer_request
min_gap_minutes        = 20
quiet_hours            = ["22:00-08:00"]

[escalate]                      # fires only if the item was NOT seen in a PEEK
calendar_lead_min = 5
reminder_lead_min = 0

[interrupt_now]                 # still subject to budget, else queue+escalate
vip_senders = []                # hand-maintained, <= 5 people, direct messages only

[queue]
max_active = 12
peek_visible = 4

[drop]
senders_muted = []
bulk_markers  = ["List-Unsubscribe", "noreply@"]
```

Rules run in a fixed order:
1. mute and bulk drop
2. expired
3. `interrupt_now` if the budget allows
4. deadline: queue with an escalation time
5. queue
6. overflow eviction

`policy.decide(candidate, context) -> Decision` is a pure function. You can test it without a radio and replay it over the whole ledger whenever the rules change.

## 2. Learning substrate

Yes to a nightly review, with two changes:

- **The label is the counterfactual action** (interrupt / queue / drop), not a 1–5 utility score. It's the policy's own output space, it takes one keypress, and it gives a confusion matrix directly.
- **Hide the policy's choice until he has labeled**, so he isn't anchored by it.

At tens of items a day, about 30 labels covers nearly everything. With three keys and a context line per item, that's under 3 minutes.

Schema, added to the existing `feedback.db` and append-only like the current tables:

```sql
CREATE TABLE candidates (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY, source TEXT NOT NULL, external_id TEXT NOT NULL,
  received_at REAL NOT NULL, sender TEXT, title TEXT, body TEXT,
  deadline REAL, expires_at REAL, content_hash TEXT NOT NULL, raw_json TEXT,
  UNIQUE(source, external_id));

CREATE TABLE decisions (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY, candidate_id INTEGER NOT NULL, decided_at REAL NOT NULL,
  policy_version TEXT NOT NULL,          -- git sha + hash(policy.toml)
  action TEXT NOT NULL CHECK(action IN ('drop','queue','interrupt')),
  rule_id TEXT NOT NULL,                 -- 'vip', 'calendar_lead', 'overflow', 'bulk', ...
  propensity REAL NOT NULL DEFAULT 1.0,  -- P(action | policy); <1 only under exploration
  escalate_at REAL,
  shadow_model TEXT, shadow_probs TEXT,  -- winnow {"interrupt":..,"queue":..,"drop":..}
  context_json TEXT NOT NULL);           -- hour, worn, secs_since_headup, interrupts_today,
                                         -- queue_len, in_calendar_event, host_batt, glasses_batt

CREATE TABLE impressions (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY, candidate_id INTEGER NOT NULL, shown_at REAL NOT NULL,
  surface TEXT NOT NULL CHECK(surface IN ('peek','item','detail','alert')),
  position INTEGER,                      -- row in PEEK; NULL elsewhere
  pulled INTEGER NOT NULL,               -- 1 = wearer-initiated => examined by construction
  dwell_ms INTEGER,
  ended_by TEXT);                        -- head_down | tap | double_tap | hold | timeout | superseded

CREATE TABLE labels (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY, candidate_id INTEGER NOT NULL, review_id INTEGER NOT NULL,
  labeled_at REAL NOT NULL,
  action TEXT NOT NULL CHECK(action IN ('drop','queue','interrupt')),
  timing TEXT CHECK(timing IN ('right','too_early','too_late')),
  note TEXT,
  sampled_because TEXT NOT NULL);        -- interrupt | disagreement | drop_sample | queue_sample

CREATE TABLE reviews (id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY, started_at REAL, ended_at REAL,
                      n_presented INTEGER, n_labeled INTEGER);
```

Questions you ask the wearer go in the same ledger (`source='ask'`, `rule_id='wearer_request'`), so all traffic is logged one way. Existing `cards` link to candidates through a new `candidate_id` column.

**Review order** (`python main.py review`, keys `i`/`q`/`d`, `t` cycles timing, `n` adds a note):
1. every interrupt
2. every item where rules and winnow disagree
3. a random sample of drops
4. a random sample of queued items

Stop at 30.

**"Learning" in the first month means no training.** About 200 labels a week is enough to:
- tune a handful of rule parameters and the VIP list by hand
- measure winnow zero-shot
- try winnow with 5–10 labeled examples in its prompt

Consider logistic regression on about 10 context features after roughly 800 labels. Don't add a vector DB. SQLite, plus FTS5 if search is ever needed.

## 3. The candidate stream

Adapters poll and write normalized rows into `candidates`. Deduplicate on `(source, external_id)` and `content_hash`. Ranked by evidence per hour of work:

1. **Calendar via the private ICS URL.** No OAuth. It's the one source with real deadlines, so it exercises escalation on day one.
2. **Gmail via IMAP with an app password.** This brings volume and realistic junk, and junk is what tests the drop path. The `List-Unsubscribe` header handles most bulk mail deterministically.
3. **Reminders he sets by asking** ("remind me at 3"). These go through the existing pipeline and become candidates with a deadline.
4. **Async answers:** a long question finishes and gets queued instead of pushed.
5. **Phone notifications.** The best source, but it needs a phone-side forwarder, which depends on the host question below. Defer it.

## 4. What runs where

At tens of items a day, **model inference energy is negligible.** winnow only reads logits for a few hundred tokens. Even at about 5 s and 7 W on a Pi 5 CPU, 50 items cost about 0.5 Wh a day. The constant costs dominate: the host's idle draw, the BLE link with its 10 s heartbeat, and the network radio. So the host is chosen by idle power, and only the generative step goes off-device.

- **Week 1:** a laptop running native Linux or Windows Python, not WSL2. He wears the glasses around the home or office within BLE range. Caveat: this doesn't test interruptions while walking outdoors. I accept that, because the week-1 questions below don't depend on it.
- **Week 2 onward:** a Pi 5 with 8 GB and a 10,000 mAh bank (about 37 Wh at roughly 3 W idle, so 10–12 h). It runs BlueZ, bleak, the rules and winnow. Stage 1 and 2 generation goes to the ProArt box over Tailscale through the phone's hotspot. A Pi Zero 2 W lasts about 30 h but can't hold the e4b model, so it only works if all models are remote. Check that `ollaya` runs on aarch64 before buying anything.
- **iPhone vs Android is still open,** and it decides whether phone notifications are ever reachable.

**Degradation ladder** (logged as host state in `context_json`):

| Level | Trigger | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| `FULL` | normal | everything on |
| `NO_NET` | no network | drill-down shows the item body cut down by `enforce_display_budget` (the fallback already exists in `run_query`); winnow stays local |
| `HOST_LOW` | host battery <20% | winnow shadow off, rules only; adapters poll every 5 min instead of every 60 s |
| `GLASSES_LOW` | glasses battery <15% | `ALERT` only; `PEEK` shrinks to the count line |
| `BLE_LOST` | link drops | candidates keep logging and the queue persists in SQLite; resync on reconnect |

## 5. Build order, ranked by what de-risks the most per hour

| # | Work | Est. | What it settles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Hardware capture.** Run `main.py --verbose` with real glasses and record raw `0xF5` traffic. Does head-up reach our connection? Does the firmware dashboard cover our `0x4E` text? How long does text stay on? Do taps in `IDLE` arrive? | 1–2 h | Whether the pull design works at all, and which gesture opens `PEEK` |
| **1** | `ledger.py` (schema above) and `review.py` | 3–4 h | Whether the label loop is bearable; works even with fake candidates |
| **2** | `sources/calendar_ics.py` and `sources/imap.py` | 3 h | That the item stream is real |
| **3** | `policy.py` with `policy.toml`, and the `attention.py` state machine | 4–6 h | That push and pull really reduce to one mechanism |
| **4** | winnow shadow scoring written to `decisions.shadow_probs` | 1–2 h | Whether a model gate beats rules |

**First milestone: steps 0–3, with step 4 if time allows, then a week of wear.** Decide the architecture against these limits, fixed before the week starts:

- **Is pull used?** At least 5 peeks a day, and at least 60% of queued items seen before they expire. Below that, pull is starving him and the queue needs more push.
- **Do interrupts earn their cost?** Among labels for items the policy interrupted, at least 80% say "interrupt". Missed interrupts (labeled "interrupt" but policy didn't) at most 1 a day.
- **Is the drop path safe?** At most 10% of sampled drops get labeled "interrupt" or "queue".
- **Does the label loop last?** Reviews completed on at least 5 of 7 nights, median under 3 minutes.
- **Model vs rules:** compare winnow's AUC with the rules' on about 150–200 labels. Be honest about the limit: the confidence interval is roughly ±0.08, so a week only detects a large difference. If winnow isn't clearly better, rules stay in charge and winnow just orders the queue.
